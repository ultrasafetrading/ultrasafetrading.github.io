IT ASSET RECOVERY

Preparing to sell your surplus IT equipment

A little preparation can make it easier to describe your equipment accurately and receive a useful assessment.

When a business replaces computers, servers, networking equipment or other technology, the older equipment can quickly become difficult to track. Before contacting a buyer, gather a clear picture of what is available.

Build a simple equipment list

  • Record the equipment type, brand and model where visible.
  • Estimate the quantity of each item and note accessories or included parts.
  • Describe the condition honestly, including devices that are damaged or incomplete.
  • Add photos of labels and overall condition where practical.

Consider data and access

Identify devices that may contain company or personal information. Follow your organisation's approved data-retention, backup and secure-erasure procedures before transferring equipment. Do not share passwords or confidential information in an enquiry.

Plan the handover

Confirm the collection location, access requirements, preferred timing and the person coordinating the handover. A clear inventory and collection plan help everyone understand the scope.

BUSINESS TECHNOLOGY

Planning a smoother office IT upgrade

A well-organised refresh considers both the new technology and the equipment being replaced.

Office technology upgrades can involve many moving parts: user requirements, device selection, data migration, installation and the retirement of older equipment. Planning these workstreams together can reduce last-minute confusion.

Start with an inventory and timeline

  • List devices by department, user or location.
  • Identify which equipment will be reused, redeployed, sold or retired.
  • Agree who is responsible for backups, data migration and sign-off.
  • Schedule collection and removal so it does not interrupt business operations.

Protect information and continuity

Confirm backup and data-handling responsibilities with your IT team. Keep records of equipment transfers and follow internal security policies before devices leave your control.

Give replaced equipment a clear next step

Working devices may be suitable for reuse or resale, while damaged or obsolete items may need appropriate recovery. Sorting equipment by condition and type makes it easier to explore the right option.

RESPONSIBLE RECYCLING

Why responsible e-waste handling matters

Electronics are made from a mix of materials and components that deserve careful handling at the end of their useful life.

Phones, computers, batteries and other electronic products should not automatically be treated like ordinary household or office rubbish. Some devices can be repaired or reused; others may require specialist handling and appropriate recycling routes.

Think reuse before disposal

Check whether equipment can be repaired, refurbished, redeployed or passed on for a further useful life. Keeping suitable devices in use can reduce the need to replace them unnecessarily.

Separate equipment thoughtfully

  • Keep batteries and damaged devices aside for appropriate handling.
  • Do not dismantle hazardous or unfamiliar components without suitable expertise.
  • Keep equipment dry and secure while arranging collection.
  • Use suitable service providers and follow applicable local requirements.

Make data security part of the process

Before devices are reused or recycled, make sure data-bearing equipment is handled in line with your organisation's data-protection procedures.
